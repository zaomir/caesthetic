## 0. Purpose

This is the CAESTHETIC project-specific implementation of the global Behavioral Communication System. It lets a new chat continue the Instagram work without reconstructing the previous conversation and records the completed grid, founder-preferred response format, mechanism rotation and content constraints.

The global rules for Specificity, Self-Discovery, epistemic labels, causal test and soft CTA are binding. This playbook may tighten them for CAESTHETIC but cannot weaken them.

## 2. Required assistant response format

When the founder asks for another carousel, answer in Russian for instructions and labels; all publishable copy remains English (US market).

Use this exact interaction pattern:

1. Start with `## Карусель N — [working title]`.
2. State the psychological/editorial strategy in one or two Russian sentences.
3. Provide exactly nine slides unless the founder specifies otherwise.
4. Label each slide in Russian, for example `### Слайд 1 — Хук`.
5. Present **one editable field in one separate `text` code block**. Never combine Title, Paragraph, Checklist, CTA or Footnote in the same block.
6. Immediately after every Title block, add a plain line: `Выделить: \`exact phrase\``.
7. Keep field labels outside code blocks: `Small label`, `Tagline`, `Title`, `Paragraph`, `Checklist`, `Question`, `Flow`, `Footnote`, `Call to Action`, `Secondary CTA`.
8. Use Tagline only on Slide 1 unless the founder explicitly requests an outro tagline. Slides 2–9 may use Small label.
9. After Slide 9, add `## Caption` and put the entire caption in **one** `text` code block.
10. Do not wrap the full response in one writing block and do not place explanatory prose inside the copy blocks.

## 3. Nine-slide narrative

Default story implements `Evidence → Tension → Discovery → Agency`:

1. **Evidence + Hook** — one labelled observation, measurement or clearly illustrative scenario that opens the tension.
2. **Tension / empathy** — describe the owner's operational reality without blame or increased certainty.
3. **Promise** — state what the reader will understand or test by Slide 9.
4. **Discovery 1** — first check, event or decision rule.
5. **Discovery 2** — second check, event or decision rule.
6. **Discovery 3** — third check, event or decision rule.
7. **Discovery synthesis** — connect the checks without imposing an unsupported diagnosis.
8. **Decision implication** — reveal the constraint, blind spot or `Do Not Do`.
9. **Agency + CTA** — one controllable next step; after self-audit, only a soft CTA.

One slide = one principal idea. Title is normally 4–10 words. Paragraph is normally 18–38 words. Use micro-copy lists where a diagnostic checklist is more useful than prose.

## 4. Strategy rotation

### 4.1 Contrarian Negation

- Use the `X is not Y; it is Z` structure **only on Slide 1**.
- Resolve the contradiction on Slide 2; do not withhold the explanation for several slides.
- Before drafting, record `Expected model → Evidence → Revised model → Decision change`. If the revised causal model or changed decision is missing, use a neutral evidence/utility cover.
- Do not schedule two contrarian carousels consecutively.
- Already used prominently in Units 6 and 8, and partially in Unit 4. The next batch should open with neutral utility, evidence, specificity or self-audit formats before another contrarian cover.

### 4.2 Effort Justification / IKEA Effect

- Give actions the reader can complete immediately in 1–3 minutes without special tools.
- Let the owner record their own observation; do not impose an invented score or diagnosis.
- Use a low-friction CTA: `SAVE THIS AUDIT`, repeat the check later, or optionally request a human-verified Growth Score.
- After a self-audit use only a soft CTA; do not use payment, sales-call pressure or a hard `$2,500 Sprint` CTA.

### 4.3 Specificity Effect

- Prefer named actions, time stamps, counts, ranges or scenarios over broad marketing claims.
- Every material claim must use the global `Observed / Measured / Calculated / Benchmark / Estimated / Illustrative` classification. `Estimated` and `Illustrative` labels stay on the same slide as the claim.
- Numbers must be canonical, sourced or explicitly labelled `ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO`.
- Never present a hypothetical number as a client result or industry benchmark.
- Already used in Unit 5 through a concrete mobile/booking-path test.

### 4.4 Loss Aversion

- Reveal a non-obvious loss already occurring, not a generic future risk.
- Use a concrete number only when its epistemic label, source/method and, for calculations, inputs are available.
- Keep loss-framed content to no more than roughly 30–40% of a series and alternate it with utility/evidence formats.
- Avoid fearmongering, blame and guaranteed financial conclusions.

## 5. Caption format

After the slides, provide one English caption in a single code block:

- answer-first opening that repeats or advances the cover thesis;
- normally 90–180 words, with a modest extension only when compliance explanation requires it;
- readable micro-paragraphs or a short numbered/bulleted path;
- one useful takeaway not dependent on opening the carousel again;
- primary CTA: `Request your free Growth Score through the link in bio.`;
- self-audit/utility posts may lead with `Save this carousel` and place the Growth Score as a secondary CTA;
- 3–5 relevant hashtags maximum;
- no URL in the caption;
- do not repeat all nine slides verbatim.

## 6. Claims, privacy and brand rules

1. English, US spelling, calm operator-side voice.
2. CAESTHETIC evaluates public marketing evidence, not clinical quality.
3. AI organizes observable signals; a human analyst verifies evidence and approves the diagnosis.
4. Never invent patients, practices, metrics, rankings, reviews, quotes, testimonials, cases or outcomes.
5. Classify every material claim as `OBSERVED`, `MEASURED`, `CALCULATED`, `BENCHMARK`, `ESTIMATED` or `ILLUSTRATIVE`; preserve provenance in the evidence ledger and a visible/anchored label in the artifact.
6. `ESTIMATED`, `ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE` or `ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO` must appear in the same slide/block as the relevant claim. An estimate or hypothesis must never be presented as measured fact.
7. Do not collect or expose names, photographs, treatment details, diagnoses, payment/insurance details or other medical information in social DMs, assets or analytics.
8. Review content: no gating, sentiment-conditioned incentives, Google-review rewards, copy-paste review scripts or public confirmation that a reviewer is a patient.
9. Avoid guarantees, universal response-time claims and unqualified ROI/revenue claims.
10. Preferred terms: `may`, `can`, `check`, `compare`, `observe`, `verify`, `candidate for investigation`.
11. Product canon: `Growth Score (free) → 30-Day Growth Sprint ($2,500) → optional Growth System`; do not hard-sell the Sprint in educational carousels.
