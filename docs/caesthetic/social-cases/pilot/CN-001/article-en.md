# Three ways to inquire. One clear next step.

*A med spa simplified its consultation request and assigned responsibility for the first reply. The recorded consultation booking rate moved from about 18% to 27%.*

A single-location med spa already offered several ways to start a conversation. Its homepage presented a free consultation, an online AI skin analysis and a phone number as parallel next steps. The case focused on what happened between that initial interest and a scheduled consultation.

## Three starts, no common next step

Visitors encountered three separate prompts. The first public form or call route did not identify a response owner, and the practice needed a consistent way to measure how inquiries became consultation bookings.

The published case identified a split next step as the earliest constraint. That gave the work a specific focus: clarify what a prospective client should do and who would take responsibility once they did it.

## Why this solution was chosen

The intervention combined one consultation request with a named response owner and a two-hour first-response rule. The media budget stayed unchanged.

The sequence mattered. First came the measurement definition, then the public inquiry route, then responsibility for responding. A clearer request and an agreed response process addressed two connected parts of the same journey.

## Who changed what

CAESTHETIC mapped the homepage and contact-route calls to action, specified the single consultation request and response rule, defined the metric, and built the comparison table.

The practice was responsible for naming the person who owned new inquiries and keeping the same next step across the website, phone conversations and messages. Its team operated the inbox. The work covered the inquiry route, copy and response ownership; it did not involve an increase in paid media.

## What remained unresolved

The share of qualified inquiries with a recorded first human reply within two hours increased from about 27% to 56%: 54 of 201 before, and 123 of 220 after.

That still left inquiries without a recorded reply inside the target window. This metric alone cannot distinguish a late reply from an absent or unrecorded reply. The improvement therefore left a specific question for follow-up: what happened to those inquiries?

## How the result was measured

The primary measure was scheduled consultations divided by qualified inbound inquiries matching the advertised services. Spam, blank submissions and duplicates were excluded.

| Period | Scheduled consultations / qualified inquiries | Rounded rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 6 January–5 April 2025 | 36 / 201 | 18% |
| 5 May–2 August 2025 | 59 / 220 | 27% |

The difference was approximately nine percentage points across two 90-day windows. These are consultation bookings, rather than a measure of completed visits or revenue.

Source: verified figures from CAESTHETIC's published case record. The comparison had no holdout group. Seasonality, staffing, other channels and unpaid media could also have affected consultation volume; the numbers do not separate the effect of each individual change.

## The lesson and the proposed next step

A clear request needs a clear handoff. In this case, simplifying the public route went together with assigning responsibility and measuring the same outcome consistently.

The proposed next step is to review inquiries without a recorded reply in the first two hours and check handoffs between team members. That is a recommendation for further work, not an intervention already reported in this case.
