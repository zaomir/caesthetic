# COMPETITIVE_DECISION_ANALYSIS_STANDARD

## 1. Purpose

Competitive analysis exists to improve a business decision, not to fill a SWOT slide. Every applicable audit, Growth Score, market review, offer review or growth diagnosis must explain:

1. where a material competitor is observably stronger;
2. which advantages customers repeatedly describe;
3. which problems customers repeatedly describe;
4. which position remains open for the subject business;
5. what to repeat, improve or refuse to copy; and
6. how the comparison changes the binding constraint and highest priorities; and
7. whether a material local/global market-practice shift makes the current technology, product, protocol, price architecture or delivery model worth keeping, evaluating, piloting or replacing.

It is a cross-cutting decision layer, not a universal fifth marketing surface. A project may use its own surface model. CAESTHETIC must compare competitors through Search, Website, Social and Reputation; Cross-Surface Consistency remains separate and unscored.

## 2. Applicability and fail-closed rule

Competitive Decision Analysis is mandatory when customers have plausible alternatives and public or permissioned evidence can materially change positioning, offer, conversion, allocation or priorities.

`not_applicable` is allowed only with a written reason, for example: no meaningful substitute set, private/enterprise market with no reliable observable evidence, or a deliberately bounded internal operational review. Lack of time is not `not_applicable`; it is `insufficient_evidence` and must produce a collection task.

Historical reports are not silently rewritten. Any refreshed, republished or newly approved report must meet the current standard.

## 3. Competitor set

Default comparison set: **3–5 material alternatives**, selected by a disclosed method. Prefer a useful mix of:

- local/direct alternatives serving the same customer and use case;
- a category leader that shapes customer expectations; and
- a positioning reference that owns a relevant, distinct decision narrative.

Multi-location businesses require a branch-specific local set where geography changes the choice. A citywide or category leader may be shared, but cannot replace local alternatives.

For every competitor record:

- name and stable identifier where available;
- selection reason and competitor type;
- geography/branch scope;
- collection date and evidence references;
- limitations and any unresolved identity ambiguity.

Do not select only weak competitors or only the largest brands. The set must reflect a real customer decision.

## 4. Comparable evidence protocol

Comparisons must be reproducible and fair:

- use the same observation period, query family, geography, device/path and sampling rule where the source permits;
- disclose the review window and sample size per business;
- separate business-observed facts from reviewer-reported experiences;
- preserve source, date, method and evidence class;
- state missing, blocked, stale or non-comparable evidence explicitly;
- never infer ad effectiveness, service quality or business results from activity alone.

One review is an episode, not an established weakness. A repeated theme requires at least two independent eligible observations in the disclosed sample; stronger language requires stronger recurrence. If recurrence is not established, label the item `single report` or `insufficient repetition` and do not use it as a strategic fact.

Use the epistemic form:

> “In the reviewed sample, X of Y eligible reviews mention …”

Never write “the competitor has bad service” when the evidence is reviewer reports.

## 5. Evidence sources

Use the sources that can change the decision; reviews are important but not sufficient by themselves.

### 5.1 Customer reviews

Extract, with counts and evidence references:

- reasons for choosing;
- expected and received outcome;
- practitioner/team relationship;
- clarity of explanation;
- safety, cleanliness and confidence;
- booking/access convenience;
- emotional value;
- scheduling and waiting problems;
- communication failures;
- price transparency;
- expectation mismatch;
- staff-treatment reports;
- repeated operational problems; and
- quality and privacy-safety of owner responses.

### 5.2 Search and local discovery

Compare visibility for disclosed queries/geographies, categories, services, locations, photos/freshness, entity completeness, branded-search control and readiness to call or book.

### 5.3 Website and conversion path

Reproduce the same customer journey:

```text
need/service search → relevant page → trust/proof → price or conditions → person/location choice → enquiry/booking
```

Record explanation quality, proof, offer clarity, path steps, failures and conversion friction.

### 5.4 Social

Compare the problems turned into content, visible experts/process, proof, locally relevant themes, meaningful reactions, recency and the route to a location or next step. Engagement is context, not proof of revenue or content effectiveness.

### 5.5 Price, service and offer construction

Compare not only price, but inclusions, paid/free consultation, first step, value explanation, packages/repeat visits, process assurances and how much must be learned from staff before a decision.

### 5.6 Public advertising

Public ad libraries and visible campaigns may show funded services, target narratives, promises, landing paths and cross-surface consistency. They do not prove spend, conversion or profitability unless separately verified.

## 6. Required competitor record

Each material competitor must carry:

```text
Competitor
→ Selection reason / type / branch scope
→ Search evidence
→ Website evidence
→ Social evidence
→ Reputation evidence
→ Repeated positive themes (count / sample / window)
→ Repeated negative themes (count / sample / window)
→ Why a customer may choose this competitor
→ Observable advantage
→ Observable gap
→ Repeat / Improve / Do not copy
→ Strategic implication
→ Binding-constraint effect
→ Priority effect
→ Market-practice gap / modernization implication
→ Evidence references
→ Limitations
```

An unavailable surface remains in the record with `insufficient_evidence`; it is never silently omitted.

## 7. Required decision outputs

Every applicable decision artifact must include:

### 7.1 Comparison Matrix

Subject business plus the selected competitors across the project’s canonical decision surfaces. Each cell contains a short observable finding or `insufficient_evidence`, with drill-down evidence references. Do not collapse the matrix into an unhelpful total competitor score.

### 7.2 Competitor Cards

For every competitor show:

- why it is included;
- what works;
- what customers repeatedly value or report as frustrating;
- why a customer may choose it;
- where the subject can outperform it;
- what to repeat, improve and refuse to copy; and
- limitations.

### 7.3 Decision Summary

The final decision layer must contain evidence-backed actions:

- **Defend** — a subject advantage to preserve;
- **Close** — a material gap to close;
- **Differentiate** — an open position to own; and
- **Do not copy** — visible competitor activity that should not be funded or imitated.

Every material comparison must end in a strategic implication. If it changes neither the binding constraint nor a priority/funding decision, explain why it remains context or remove it.

### 7.4 Market Practice Gap / Strategic Modernization

The analyst must test whether the subject still operates on a materially older market practice while relevant local or global peers have adopted a different approach. This can cover:

- technology, equipment and software;
- product, material or — in regulated clinical work — drug/protocol choices;
- service design and customer journey;
- price architecture, packages and first-step offer;
- proof, education and expectation-setting;
- operating model, access, booking, follow-up and retention.

Do not use “everyone has moved on” or “outdated” without a disclosed evidence base. Newer is not automatically better. Adoption does not prove superiority, and public marketing does not prove clinical efficacy, safety, economics or customer fit.

Every material modernization recommendation contains:

```text
Current state
→ Observed local/global shift
→ Evidence scope and adoption signal
→ Customer/business relevance
→ Transition economics and operational dependencies
→ Keep | Evaluate | Pilot | Replace | Do not adopt
→ Required specialist/regulatory validation
→ Evidence references
→ Limitations
```

For regulated medical or dental practice, marketing analysis may identify a market-practice gap and its patient-decision implications. It may not declare a drug, device or clinical protocol obsolete, unsafe or clinically superior, or direct a clinical change, without appropriately qualified clinical review, regulatory fit and evidence authority. Pricing recommendations must distinguish observed market construction from verified profitability and patient affordability.

## 8. Scoring and claims

- Competitive analysis informs diagnosis, binding constraint, positioning and priorities; it does not automatically create a separate weighted score.
- Do not publish a composite “competitor score” unless its decision use, components and limitations are explicit.
- Customer language is attributed to the reviewed sample, not asserted as an operating fact.
- Public activity does not prove effectiveness.
- Correlation does not prove causation.
- Estimates and inferred positions must be labelled and include method/assumptions.

## 9. Project adapter contract

Each project marketing adapter must either:

1. reference this standard and define its market-specific competitor types, canonical surfaces, data sources, selection method and legal/platform constraints; or
2. declare `not_applicable` with a durable reason.

Projects must not copy this standard into local SSOTs. Local evidence, competitors, branch sets, thresholds required by law/platform and current decisions remain project-specific.

## 10. Acceptance gate

An applicable competitive analysis is incomplete unless all are true:

- named competitors and selection reasons exist;
- periods, queries/paths and sample rules are disclosed;
- comparable evidence is separated by surface/source;
- review themes include recurrence and sample size, or explicitly state insufficient repetition;
- each competitor states customer-choice reason, advantage, gap, action and limitation;
- Comparison Matrix, Competitor Cards and Decision Summary exist;
- `Defend / Close / Differentiate / Do not copy` have evidence references;
- Market Practice Gap is explicitly `applicable`, `no_material_gap` or `insufficient_evidence`; applicable recommendations use `Keep / Evaluate / Pilot / Replace / Do not adopt` and disclose specialist/regulatory gates;
- binding-constraint and Top-priority effects are explicit; and
- a human approves material inferences before client publication.

---

**Canonical principle:** compare to decide. Never turn competitor activity or isolated complaints into unsupported strategy.
