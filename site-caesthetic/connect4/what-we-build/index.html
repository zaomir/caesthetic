00 / The idea

Close the first plane. Then keep the language moving.

Connect4 aligns Search & Maps, Website, Social, and Reviews & Reputation. On this page we show the practical build: one agreed set of low-frequency phrases, placed where a potential client actually looks.

If the business has many locations and one core offer, we lead with a location catalog. If it has many products or services from one place, we lead with a product catalog. The homepage points to that catalog. Each catalog item gets its own page—written around the phrases people use when they search.

What stays constant: the demand language and the facts of the offer.

What changes: format—page, post, map card, directory listing, or a useful business reply.

02 / Social

A post for each location or product, then real conversation.

After the website plane is in place, we move the same phrases into social, where people meet the people and expertise behind the offer.

For each catalog item we publish a focused post that uses the same low-frequency language. Under that post we build a conversation: comments that ask with those phrases, and replies that answer with them.

That thread becomes visible user-generated context around the offer. Social is not a copy of the website. It is the same reality in a channel-native form.

Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube and other relevant accounts can all participate. The surface is Social, not a single app.

03 / Maps

Pack the map card. Invite honest reviews. Reply with care.

On Maps we create or complete the location presence and pack the business card: categories, services, photos, hours, and the right next step.

For multi-location businesses we work location by location. For a single location with many products, the card still has to make the offer findable and point to the right explanation.

Then we implement our review and feedback system: a dependable way to invite genuine reviews after a real visit. Clients choose their own words and ratings. We do not script praise or gate public reviews by satisfaction.

Where the business replies, we use natural, useful language that can include the same demand phrases, without inventing a client’s experience or confirming private details.

04 / Local platforms

Where the local audience already looks for recommendations.

The fourth plane uses local recommendation and reputation platforms that the market actually uses: niche directories, community guides, or review sites relevant to the offer.

There we place the same low-frequency language in the listing and in structured conversation where the platform allows it. The goal is consistency of meaning with the website, social, and Maps work, not a fifth disconnected channel.

In the Connect4 model this work sits inside Reviews & Reputation, alongside map reviews and business replies.

Questions

Before we start.

Do you always build a location catalog?

No. We choose a location catalog or a product/service catalog from how the business is actually organized. The homepage still needs a clear path into that catalog.

Is this the same as buying ads?

No. This page describes organic foundation work across public surfaces. Paid ads are a demand layer discussed only after the relevant path is ready. See the Connect4 explanation.

Do you write clients’ reviews for them?

No. Clients choose their own words and ratings. Our review system invites genuine feedback and helps the team respond. Business replies may use relevant demand language without claiming a client’s experience.

Can we start with one plane?

Yes. Scope follows the verified priority. We often close the website plane first, then carry the same phrase set into social, Maps, and local platforms, but the written Sprint scope decides what is included.
