How to Collect More Customer Reviews—and Understand What Needs Fixing

A public rating tells only part of the story. A useful feedback process helps customers describe their experience and gives a manager enough detail to understand what the team needs to improve.

CAESTHETIC · September 11, 2026

Give feedback a place in everyday work

If review requests depend on someone remembering at the end of a busy day, the process becomes uneven. Start by deciding who owns it, which customer interactions are in scope and how the team will know that the agreed process is being used.

A customer should be able to share an honest experience without being told what to say. Feedback may describe something excellent, a small frustration or a problem that needs a response. Each can be useful.

Public reputation and management feedback serve different needs

Public reviews help prospective customers assess experiences reported by others. Feedback addressed to the team can give a manager a clearer picture of an individual concern and the action needed. Neither channel should be treated as a substitute for improving the service itself.

For example, repeated comments about unclear arrival instructions may point to a communication problem. A manager can check the instructions, assign a correction and review whether the same concern returns. This is an illustrative workflow, not a reported client outcome.

Keep invitations fair and feedback authentic

Google requires contributions to reflect genuine experiences and prohibits incentivized reviews and attempts to manipulate ratings. Read Google’s review content policy.

Use neutral invitations. Let customers choose their own wording and rating. Do not make the opportunity to post a public review depend on positive feedback, and do not ask customers to insert service keywords. Check the rules of each reputation platform before including it in the process.

Make a concern actionable for the manager

A manager needs to know what was raised, which location it concerns, who is responsible and whether the team has followed up. Group recurring service issues so that individual messages can reveal a pattern worth investigating.

Keep public replies relevant and respectful. In a practice setting, use the practice’s approved privacy approach and move individual care discussions to an appropriate private channel. A public reply should not disclose sensitive details.

Measure the process as well as the rating

  • Adoption: is the agreed invitation and follow-up process being used?
  • Feedback: what themes recur, and at which locations?
  • Response: do concerns receive an owner and a recorded next step?
  • Reputation: how do review volume, recency and ratings change over a defined period?

Record a baseline before launch. A higher average rating alone does not prove that every operational issue has been resolved, and a new concern can be a useful signal even when the public rating remains strong.
