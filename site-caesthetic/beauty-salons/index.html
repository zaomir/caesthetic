OWNER PROBLEM

A salon can look busy online and still leave gaps in the calendar.

The useful question is where demand stops becoming a booked and returning client — not which channel to buy next.

Social looks active. Why are appointment slots still empty?

Should we buy more advertising, or repair booking and rebooking first?

Which location, service or specialist loses the most demand?

Why do first-time clients fail to become regulars?

FOUR SURFACES

Exactly four surfaces clients cross-check before they choose.

Different channels have different jobs, but they must describe one salon without contradictions and lead to a compatible next action.

01 · Search / Maps

Local discovery

Identity, branches, services, hours, freshness and the route from the listing to booking.

02 · Website / Booking

The booking path

Service → specialist → location → available time → booking, without incompatible links.

03 · Social

Choice, then action

Whether current work helps a client choose a service or specialist and move to booking.

04 · Reputation / Reviews

Independent proof

Review freshness, volume, branches, services, specialists and owner replies.

Paid Ads amplify the system. They do not repair it. Paid advertising is the Demand Layer, not a fifth surface. Cross-Surface Consistency is a check across the four surfaces, not a fifth surface.

CLIENT DECISION JOURNEY

The route, not the channel list, names the first constraint.

A new client, a returning client and a multi-location client do not fail in the same place.

New client

Discovery to first confirmation

Discovery → proof → price and location → available time → confirmation.

Returning client

Visit to the next visit

Visit → rebooking prompt → reminder → direct booking → return.

Multi-location

One brand, one booking system

Brand → nearest location → available specialist → consistent service → one booking system.

NEW / ESTABLISHED / MULTI-LOCATION

The same method. Different starting evidence.

The Score does not invent a growth stage. It reads the public route that actually exists.

New salon

Can a first client complete the path?

Identity, booking continuity and proof that the offer is real enough to book.

Established salon

Where does known demand leak?

Usually booking, rebooking or a contradiction between surfaces that already attract attention.

Multi-location

Does each branch keep the same route?

Hours, services, specialists and booking must stay compatible across locations.

OPERATIONAL FLOOR

The operating floor matters — and is not guessed from outside.

Enquiry → response → booking → confirmation → attendance → service → rebooking → return.

CRM, telephony, reception, chatbot, reminders, no-shows, rebooking and staff capacity may be relevant. They are interventions against a verified constraint, not a public catalogue of CAESTHETIC services.
